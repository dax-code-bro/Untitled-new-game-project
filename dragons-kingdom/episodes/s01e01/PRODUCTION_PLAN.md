# Dragon's Kingdom, Episode 1: Production Plan

**Nothing from Episode 1 has been rendered yet. This is a plan, not a movie.**

Part 1 is for Daxtyn. Part 2 has the details for whoever builds it.

## Part 1: for Daxtyn

### Where we are right now

What exists:

- **Your screenplay** (`screenplay.md`). Everything follows it.
- **Planning files** in this folder:
  - every spoken line (`dialogue.md`);
  - every camera shot (`shotlist.md`);
  - everything that has to be built (`assets.md`);
  - voices, sound and music (`audio-plan.md`).
- **A 4K video maker** that is being built right now (the `render/` folder). It has only made short test clips of a test castle. That is not episode footage.
- **Small sound tests**: computer voices for every role, 7 test sound effects, 2 short music sketches. The sound files were made in a temporary folder and are not in the project, but the scripts that made them are saved in `tools/audio-prototypes/`.

What does **not** exist yet:

- No episode footage. Not one shot of Episode 1 has been rendered.
- No 3D models of Remi, Abby, the dragons, Verdor or Cling.
- No animatic (the rough test version of the episode).
- No recorded voices, no finished music, no finished sound.

### How long will Episode 1 be?

| | Length |
|---|---|
| Shortest likely | 20 min 36 s |
| **Expected** | **25 min 16 s** |
| Longest likely | 31 min 58 s |
| The screenplay's plan | 50 min |

- The story as written fills about **25 minutes** when told at a natural speed.
- That is a normal length for an animated TV episode.
- **We will not stretch it to 50 minutes.** Your screenplay says no repeated shots, frozen pictures or slow talking just to hit a number. We agree.
- If you want a longer episode, the way to get there is new scenes, not slower ones.
- The real length will be known after the animatic (step B).

### What it will look like (honest)

- **Stylized 3D.** Think of a good video-game cutscene, not a Pixar movie and not a live-action film.
- Everything is built from code and drawn by the computer's processor. This computer has no graphics card.

What will look good:

- Big landscapes, the sea, the sky, sunlight and mist.
- Dragons flying: big shapes moving against the sky.
- **Size**: tiny Remi on huge Charcoal; Leaf next to Charcoal; Starlight over the village.
- Wide shots of the Verdor coast and of Cling.
- Silhouettes, like the scout's wing tearing loose against bright water.

What is hardest:

- **Faces up close** (43 shots) and **mouths matching the words** (97 shots).
- Hands, hair and clothes moving.
- **Crowds** in Cling (20 shots).
- The wet gold hatchling, dust, and breaking roofs.
- Starlight's white scales. They must not look flat or glowing.

How we keep the hard parts good:

- Keep faces simple, and the same in every shot.
- Use medium shots, backs and silhouettes where the screenplay allows.
- Match mouths to the real recorded voices with a free tool.
- Keep crowds in the background, using a few repeated body types.

**One honest warning.** Your screenplay asks for "realistic cinematic fantasy". This way of making it cannot fully reach "realistic". It will look stylized. The screenplay asks us to say so instead of hiding it, so we are saying it now.

### The plan, step by step

**A. Look pictures (style frames).**
- You get about 8 still pictures in 4K: the three dragon sizes side by side, Charcoal and Leaf flying, Starlight above Cling, the gold hatchling, the Verdor coast, Cling square, a face test of Remi, Abby and Alexandria, and the scout.
- You say yes, or what to change. Every design is temporary until you approve it.

**B. Animatic (rough version).**
- The whole episode as a small, rough video with simple models and computer voices.
- About 3 hours of computer time.
- You watch it and say what feels too slow, too fast or unclear. It shows the real length.

**C. Build.**
- Make the real people, dragons, places and effects.
- Real voices are recorded during this step.

**D. Final 4K render.**
- One scene at a time, in 10-second pieces. If something crashes, it carries on from where it stopped.

**E. Sound, subtitles and checks.**
- Mix the voices, effects and music. Add subtitles.
- Watch the whole video and check it against your screenplay's checklist.
- Only then do we call it finished.

### How long the computer needs

These speeds were **measured** on this computer.

| What | Time for the whole episode |
|---|---|
| Animatic (small size) | about 3 hours |
| **Final 4K, our suggested mix** | **about 15-18 hours (maybe 19-22 for busy scenes)** |
| Final 4K, every frame at full 4K | about 23-26 hours (maybe 28-32 for busy scenes) |

- Fixing and re-rendering adds more. Plan for about **30-43 hours of computer time in total** (1-2 days), spread over many nights.
- Building and animating 329 shots takes much longer than rendering them.
- Space needed: about **15-18 GB**. This computer has about 26 GB free.
- This computer is temporary. Finished videos must be saved somewhere safe.

### Voices

- We tested free computer voices on this computer. They work, and each role gets its own voice.
- They are good enough for the animatic and for small roles.
- They **cannot** really whisper, cry out in pain, shout, or sound like a child.
- **Suggestion:** you, your family or friends record **Abby, Remi, Alexandria and the King of Cling**. You could read the **Narrator** yourself. A phone or a cheap USB microphone is enough. `audio-plan.md` explains how.
- Ask everyone if their voice can be used. Anyone under 18 needs a parent's OK.

### Decisions only you can make

1. **Length.** Is about 25 minutes OK for Episode 1? (We suggest yes.)
2. **Looks.** Do you have drawings or pictures of the people and dragons? If not, we make temporary designs for step A. Also: do the dragons have four legs and two wings, or are the wings their front legs?
3. **Sizes.** In step A you will see three size options for Leaf, Charcoal and Starlight. Pick A, B or C. (We suggest B, "how big they look".)
4. **Voices.** Who records which role? And how do you say "Bashion", "Verdor" and "Tara"?
5. **Quality or speed.** Is our mix OK? It uses full 4K for close-ups and big moments, and sharpened 4K plus "twos" for calm talking shots. Or should every frame be full 4K? That takes about a day per render.
6. **Suggested story parts.** Your screenplay marks these as suggestions. Keep them?
   - Abby's LEFT arm is the hurt one.
   - Starlight makes a second pass over Cling.
   - The escape and survivor moments.
   - The ending order "send word to King Fallen".
7. **Posting and credits.** Will you post it publicly or earn money from it? (Our plan only uses tools and voices that allow both.) Whose names go in the end credits?
8. **Small choices.** Part 2 lists the choices we made for you: the narrator's voice, accents, captions, music, subtitles and more. Are they OK? Change any you like.

### Things we chose for you

The screenplay says that anything not in its CANON LOCK is a suggestion until you accept it. The biggest ones:

- Abby's **left** arm; the scout loses its **left** wing (that one is canon).
- The flying shots go left to right. The scout zips **between Leaf and Charcoal**.
- Cling's map: the arch on the left, the big road on the right, a **fountain with a stone pillar** in the middle.
- Dragon sizes "as they look on screen" (until you pick).
- Computer voices for the animatic, with a woman's voice for the Narrator.
- Music and sound effects made in code.

Part 2, section 7 has the full list.

### 2. Runtime

From `shotlist.json` → `totals`: expected = dialogue time + action time + holds, at a natural pace.

| | Seconds | mm:ss |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low (tight cutting) | 1,236.5 | 20:36 |
| **Expected** | **1,515.5** | **25:16** |
| High (slower performances, longer holds) | 1,918.5 | 31:58 |
| Screenplay editorial plan | 3,000 | 50:00 |

- **Breakdown:** speech 6:47 (7:57 with cue gaps), action 16:08, holds and breaths 1:09. End titles are counted at 40 s.
- **Every scene is 38-69% of its editorial window.** The screenplay calls those windows "an editorial plan, not measured footage".
- **No padding.** The screenplay forbids repeats, frozen frames and slowed speech to reach a number. Holds are only the screenplay's own pauses plus small 0.5-1 s breaths; 1D-26 has none because the screenplay says "keep it brief".
- **The animatic sets the truth.** TTS speaks 30-45% faster than the timing estimates (`audio-plan.md`), and human actors will differ again. Replace estimates with measured takes.
- **If Daxtyn wants ~50 minutes,** that means new written scenes. Writing them is a story decision for him, and it must not move Episode 2 material forward (CANON LOCK 16).

### 3. Quality ceiling and how to work within it

The method is procedural three.js, rendered on CPU (SwiftShader) in headless Chromium. Blender Cycles measured 101 s/frame and was rejected.

- **Expected look:** stylized real-time 3D (game-cutscene level). Standard PBR materials, shadow maps, an environment-map sky, fog and sprite-based volumetrics. No path tracing, no planar reflections by default, no true volumetric clouds (`assets.json` → `render_budget_guidelines`).
- **Strong areas:** terrain, ocean and sky; scale staging (long lenses, humans for scale, slow big dragons and fast small ones); silhouettes against bright water and sky; wide establishing shots.
- **Risk areas,** with counts from `shotlist.json` → `totals.shots_per_tag`:

  | Risk | Shots |
  |---|---:|
  | FACE_CLOSEUP | 43 |
  | LIPSYNC | 97 |
  | CROWD | 20 |
  | DUST | 33 |
  | DESTRUCTION | 8 |
  | HATCHLING_SOFTBODY | 11 |
  | CLOTH | 14 |

  Also: the head-in-mouth beat (2 shots), wing separation, the fast pass, fire, and water impact.

- **Faces are the biggest risk** (`assets.md`). Port the sculpted head and its 13 shapes from Daxtyn's old engine (`engine/src/91-face.js`), then add what it lacks:
  - moving eyes;
  - a mouth interior;
  - fingers;
  - per-character face shape;
  - brows and hair.

  Prove one head at preview resolution in stage A, before anything else depends on it.
- **Lip-sync:** take mouth cues from the final WAVs with Rhubarb Lip Sync (MIT; not installed yet, a free download).
- **Limitation to flag (RENDER-HANDOFF CHECKLIST):** "realistic cinematic fantasy" (PRODUCTION CONTINUITY) will be met as stylized fantasy, not photorealism. No established event changes because of this.

  - every flying shot on ones.

#### E. Audio, subtitles and end-to-end QA

- **Audio mix:** follow `audio-plan.md`.
  - Stems DX / FX / BG / FOLEY / MX at 48 kHz, 24-bit.
  - Master −16 LUFS integrated, ≤ −2 dBTP, exactly frames × 2000 samples long, starting at frame 0.
  - Mux with `render.mjs --audio` (AAC 256 kbps). The pipeline does not resample.
- **Subtitles:** from `dialogue.json` → `subtitle_cues` and the placed takes. Deliver a sidecar `.srt` plus a soft `mov_text` track (no re-encode). Keep place captions out of the subtitle area.
- **QA checklist** (screenplay RENDER-HANDOFF CHECKLIST and FINAL CONTINUITY CHECK, plus technical checks). Tick every item on the real file:
  - [ ] One reference model and one voice per recurring character, across every sequence. No new face for Remi, Abby, Alexandria, Queen Fall or the King of Cling between shots.
  - [ ] Same riding equipment before and after close-ups.
  - [ ] Weather, sun direction (Verdor: sun on the sea side; Cling: behind the steps) and coastline positions match across cuts.
  - [ ] Abby's injury is her LEFT arm from 1E-04 on (sling from 2C-12). The scout loses its LEFT wing (1F-20 on). No mirrored shots; 1E follows `conventions.the_pass_1E`.
  - [ ] All four [ORIGINAL] lines are exact; no source marker or bracket note is spoken or shown.
  - [ ] Subtitles come from the final spoken dialogue, not production notes.
  - [ ] Shot lengths come from performance and readable movement, not loops or freezes.
  - [ ] Canon beats:
    - Abby is silent after L046.
    - Leaf starts the nip, and Charcoal releases him unharmed.
    - The fracture comes from the pass (no weapon, bite or fall).
    - "Attack" never triggers flame; fire comes only after "Fire".

    - Starlight has no wounds and no fire; Queen Fall is never labelled Alexandria.
    - No Water Gliders and no Terrence/Reginald fleet battle.
  - [ ] Provisional designs are labelled. Any change forced by a production limit is flagged, not silently rewritten.
  - [ ] Technical:
