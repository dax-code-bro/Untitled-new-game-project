# Dragon's Kingdom S01E01: work-in-progress preview, native 4K render plan

> **Plan only.** Nothing has been rendered for this preview yet, and the scene files it names have not been written. Do not start until the model-quality workflow has finished: it owns `scenes/lib/creatures`, `humans`, `architecture`, `nature`, `props` and `scenes/lookdev`, and every render job fingerprints those files. The seconds-per-frame figures are style-frame measurements where they say MEASURED, and estimates derived from them everywhere else.

Generated by `tools/render_plan.py` from `edl.json` (00:09:59:21, 14397 frames). The job list is in `render-jobs.json`.

## The answer

- **14,235 frames to render at 3840x2160.** That is the 14,229 picture frames minus the black P-01, plus 102 dissolve-handle frames. The two cards, the captions, the title and the corner tag are drawn in the conform, not rendered.
- **About 168 hours of rendering for one browser** using the first-frame figures below (range 125-207 h).
- **With 2 workers on this 4-thread machine: about 154 hours (6.4 days).** Expect 131 h if steady-state frames run 15 % faster than first frames, which the pilot will measure. The full range is 97-189 h. Browser start-up and scene setup add about 4.9 h over 129 jobs.
  - Two browsers do not halve the time on this machine. SwiftShader already spreads one browser across all 4 threads; the README benchmark measured 0.466 frames/s for 1 browser and 0.511 for 2, a 1.10x gain.
  - On **two separate machines** like this one, splitting the jobs by set brings it to about 77 h (3.2 days), or less on machines with more cores.
- **The look is native 4K throughout.** There is no `final-fast` upscale and no animation on twos.
  - All running footage uses the measured style-frame finish with velocity motion blur.
  - The pass (1E-03) uses 8 real sub-frames, because velocity blur assumes straight motion.
- **Biggest costs:** the birthing chamber, the sky and the riding grounds; see the table. The two sets with no measurement, BIRTHING_CHAMBER and SKY_OFF_VERDOR, are over half the total. Measuring them in the pilot is the first thing to do.

## Scene files and libraries per set

Each set becomes one shot-driven scene in `episodes/s01e01/preview/scenes/` (to be written in the next step).

- **Camera, lens, timing and blocking** come from `edl.json` through a generated shot table (`scenes/<set>.shots.json`). The scene loads it in `setup()`, so render jobs fingerprint it.
- **`update(t)`** looks up the shot by `round(t*24)` in its own set timeline, then poses everything for `t`, as a pure function of `t`.
- **The look** is the style frame's look (`filmFinish`), rendered with `--cinematic velocity`.

## Cheapest tricks that keep the look

Only tricks that change nothing visible are in this list. Each one is checked with an A/B still before it is used: render one frame both ways at 4K and compare. A difference anyone could see means the trick is dropped.

1. **Velocity motion blur instead of 8 sub-frames for running footage.** This is already in the figures above.
   - Measured: F1 went from 39 s to 12.7 s and F2 from 253 s to 50.7 s per frame, with the same grade, grain and lens.
   - Only 1E-03 keeps 8 sub-frames, because its curved high-speed pass would blur wrongly with velocity blur.
2. **Do not render what is not 3D.** P-01 black, both cards, the five place captions, the title, the corner tag and every dissolve are done in the conform.
   - That saves 264 frames.
   - A text change never forces a re-render.
3. **Shadow cache (`ctx.shadows`) on locked-off shots.** It is bit-identical by construction (`npm test`).
   - The chamber is locked off in 28 of its 35 shots, and the riding grounds in most of 1B. Static walls, terrain, the nest and the grounded dragons' bodies are drawn into the shadow map once per shot.
   - Only moving casters go in `dynamic`: breathing flanks, people, the hatchling.
   - Measured saving: 50-90 ms per frame in test-kingdom, more where there is more static geometry. That is small (~2-4 %) but free.
4. **Grass LOD by distance.** F2 draws all 77k tufts in three layers in every frame (`grass.js` has no LOD; each layer is one instanced mesh).
   - Split each layer into distance tiles with `dk/instancing.js` tiled instancing, so off-screen tiles are skipped. That trick took the test-kingdom forest from 570 to 190 ms per draft frame.
   - Beyond ~40 m, use a 3-blade tuft instead of a 7-12-blade one. A 4K pixel covers several blades there, and the blades' sky-bent normals keep the shading the same.
   - Expected saving: 15-25 % on the grounds shots, the biggest single saving available (to be measured). It must pass an A/B still at the 1B-02 end frame and the 1C-01 low angle.
5. **Switch off what a shot cannot see.**
   - Shots facing inland, and every chamber shot, skip `ocean.update(t)`: 125 ms of CPU for the FFT, twice with velocity blur.
   - They also skip the ocean late pass (about 1 s per 4K frame when water fills the view).
   - Sets that are behind the camera are left out of the scene graph for that shot.
   - Nothing visible changes, because the camera cannot see them. Check reflections: the ocean reflects only what is on screen anyway.
6. **Render the sky background once, only where nothing in it moves.**
   - Most locked-off shots do not qualify. The sea moves, the clouds drift, grain changes every frame, the window-shaft fog drifts, and depth of field and fog need the full depth buffer.
   - The runtime has no plate-plus-holdout compositing today, so this trick is **not used**. Adding it would need a runtime change and its own bit-exact test.
   - The sky is already cheap: it is drawn last, so only uncovered pixels pay for it.
7. **One browser setup per shot job, two browsers per job.** Both browsers take chunks from the same shot, so the scene builds (up to ~45 s for the heavy sets) are paid twice per shot, not per chunk.
8. **Measure before committing.** The pilot (below) replaces the estimates with steady-state numbers, and the plan is recomputed with `render_plan.py`.

Rejected because they change the look:

- `final-fast`: renders at 1440p and upscales.
- `--twos`: stylises the motion and freezes the grain on pairs of frames.
- Fewer depth-of-field or motion-blur taps.
- Lower fog-froxel resolution: visible blocks in the light shafts at 4K.
- Smaller shadow maps.
- Lowering the ocean cascade resolution: needs an A/B test first and is not counted.

## Job and chunk layout (a restart loses at most one chunk per worker)

- **One `render.mjs` job per shot: 129 jobs.** Each job has its own folder in `output/preview-s01e01/<shot>/`; `output/` is git-ignored.
  - A finished shot stays valid when another shot is fixed later.
  - Editing a scene or asset invalidates only the jobs that have not finished, and `render.mjs` refuses to mix versions inside one video.
- **Set timeline.** Inside its set scene, each shot starts on a whole second, at least 1 s after the previous shot plus its handles.
  - `--start` is that whole second and `--seconds` is frames/24, so `render.mjs`'s rounding gives exactly the planned frames.
  - Choosing the shot by `round(t*24)` keeps the shutter-open sample of each frame (t - 1/96 s) inside the right shot.
- **Chunks.** 2 s chunks (48 frames) for shots under 20 s per frame, 1 s (24 frames) up to 100 s per frame, and 0.5 s (12 frames) for the pass.
  - No chunk is more than about 25 minutes of one browser's work.
  - `render.mjs` encodes each chunk, decodes it completely to check it, records its size and SHA-1, and only then marks it done.
  - **If the container restarts, run the same queue command again.** Finished jobs are skipped, and the interrupted job resumes after its last verified chunk.
  - At most the chunk each of the 2 workers had in progress is lost: 2 x 24 frames on the chamber, about 20 minutes.
- **Workers.** `--workers 2` per job.
  - Each 4K browser needs about 2.6 GB on test-kingdom; the heavy sets need more (the pilot measures it).
  - If two browsers do not fit in the 15 GB, use `--workers 1` and lose about 9 % of the throughput.
- **Output format.** Intermediates are written with `--bit-depth 10 --crf 12`: High 10, so smooth skies survive the second encode in the conform. Expect roughly 4-8 GB for all shots, since film grain is expensive to encode. Check `df` first.
- **Order.**
  1. Pilot: 24 frames each of P-03, 1A-04, 1A-28, 1B-02, 1D-10 and 1E-03, with `--timing` on one frame each. These measure steady-state seconds per frame and peak RAM.
  2. Recompute this plan.
  3. Render all jobs in EDL order, so a partial render is always a watchable beginning.
- The queue runner (next step) reads `render-jobs.json`. It runs each `command` from `dragons-kingdom/`, skips jobs whose `<shot>.mp4` exists and passes `ffprobe`, and logs the s/frame of every job back into the plan.

Example: the first job of each set.

```
node render/render.mjs episodes/s01e01/preview/scenes/sea-coast-dawn.js --preset final --cinematic velocity --start 0 --seconds 6.000000 --workers 2 --chunk-seconds 2.0000 --bit-depth 10 --crf 12 --out output/preview-s01e01/P-02
node render/render.mjs episodes/s01e01/preview/scenes/prologue-vistas.js --preset final --cinematic velocity --start 0 --seconds 6.833333 --workers 2 --chunk-seconds 2.0000 --bit-depth 10 --crf 12 --out output/preview-s01e01/P-08
node render/render.mjs episodes/s01e01/preview/scenes/riding-grounds.js --preset final --cinematic velocity --start 0 --seconds 7.500000 --workers 2 --chunk-seconds 1.0000 --bit-depth 10 --crf 12 --out output/preview-s01e01/P-17
```

## Conform (after the shots exist)

1. **Picture.** One ffmpeg pass builds the timeline from `edl.json`:
   - Trim each shot's handles. Use `xfade` for the prologue dissolves (centred on the cut) and fade P-02 in from black.
   - Generate P-01 black and the two cards.
   - Draw the captions, the title and the corner tag with `drawtext`, using the EDL frames and fades.
   - Encode once, in two versions:
     - an 8-bit CRF 16 master that plays everywhere;
     - a 10-bit CRF 16 master for YouTube.
   - x264 at 4K runs at a few frames per second here, so allow 1-2 h.
2. **Sound.** The mix places each take at `take_start_frame x 2000` samples.
   - L003 goes in as its five parts. Gains and spaces come from `takes.json`, with the beds, cues and music from `edl.json`.
   - The master is 48 kHz, 24-bit and exactly `frames x 2000` samples, loudness-normalised as in `audio-plan.md`. It is muxed as AAC 256k, with the video stream copied.
3. **Subtitles.** A soft English track is generated from `edl.json` line times and `dialogue.json` `subtitle_cues`.
4. **Checks.** The video is exactly 14397 frames at 24 fps and 3840x2160, and the audio is exactly 28,794,000 samples.
   - Extract stills at every cut, at frames 0-71 (the card) and at the last 96 frames.
   - Re-run `check_edl.py`.

## Risks

- **The estimates are unmeasured for the two largest sets.** The chamber and the sky could each move the total by ±20 h. The pilot settles this.
- **The scene files are not written yet.** They are a sizeable piece of work, mostly assembling existing pieces per shot.
- **Faces are deliberately unreadable.** Every line is staged without a readable mouth, and five face beats are carried by the body. This is the right call while lip-sync does not exist, but the preview will feel more distant than the finished episode should.
- **The machine is shared.** The model-quality workflow runs heavy jobs, and these figures assume the CPU is free.
