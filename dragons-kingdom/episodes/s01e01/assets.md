# Dragon's Kingdom, Season 1 Episode 1: What Has to Be Built

This is the list of everything the computer has to build for Episode 1: people, dragons, places and effects. Every visual piece is made from code; no copyrighted art or sound is downloaded. The full details for the build are in `assets.json`.

**Nothing has been built or rendered for this episode yet.** This is a plan. Every face, dragon body and place design here is **provisional**: it is a placeholder until you send pictures or say what they should look like. No ages, permanent face features, personal names or coats of arms have been made up.

## The short version

- **21 kinds of people** to build. 5 need faces good enough for big close-ups: Remi, Abby, Alexandria, Queen Fall and the King of Cling. 12 more have speaking parts, so their mouths must move with the words.
- **5 dragons**: Charcoal, Leaf, Starlight, the gold hatchling and the Slitherwing scout. There is also an unnamed shadow in the prologue mist.
- **7 places**: the prologue sea and coast, the birthing chamber, the riding field, the sky and sea off Verdor, the treatment room, the harbor with the Santa Maria, and the village of Cling.
- **27 effects**, such as dust, flying turf, Charcoal's fire, splashes, mist, breaking roofs and flapping banners.
- Your **old engine** has parts worth reusing: the sculpted human head with its expressions, the body builder, the grass, and the animal-body tricks. That saves weeks of work. Details are below.

## The size problem

You said Leaf is about one-twentieth of Charcoal, and that Starlight is about twice Charcoal. "Size" can mean three different things, and they give very different pictures. The table uses a made-up working length of 8 m for Leaf, just for comparing.

| If "size" means… | Charcoal | Starlight | Remi on Charcoal | Starlight's wings vs Cling's square |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bulk or weight | about 22 m long | about 27 m | easy to see | about as wide as the square |
| **how big they look on screen** (suggested) | about 36 m | about 51 m | "a small fixed point", still visible | about 1.6 times the square |
| length | about 160 m | about 320 m | a speck (about 1% of Charcoal's length) | 10 times the square: she would flatten the whole village |

The screenplay's own scenes fit the middle option best. With the bulk option, Charcoal is less than three times Leaf's length, which may not look "dramatically smaller". The length option breaks several scenes:
- Abby has to stay just outside Charcoal's mouth.
- Remi has to stay visible as "a small fixed point".
- Starlight is "a white expanse between roofs", and the stone arch survives her attack.

Starlight and Charcoal are never in the same shot in this episode, so her size only shows against the village.

The plan: the build uses one setting for all sizes. We make **one lineup picture** of all three options, with a person for scale, and you choose. Whatever you pick stays a production choice, not a new canon number.

Tricks that make a big size difference read on screen:
- put the small dragon in front and the big one behind it;
- always include a person for scale;
- let big dragons move slowly while small ones move fast;
- use shadows (Charcoal's shadow swallowing the scout);
- let a huge wing fill the whole frame.

## Effects

- **Ground and air:** rolling dust, flying turf and stones, grass bending under wingbeats, the scar in the field.
- **Fire:** Charcoal's short fire breath, the rider's cloth scorching, a smoke trail. **No fire in Cling at all.**
- **Water and sky:** the open sea, the scout's splash and trail, sea mist, prologue fog, clouds Starlight comes out of.
- **Village:** breaking tiles and timber, a big dust cloud, falling dust afterwards, flapping and tearing banners, a loose metal piece banging in the wind.
- **Small things:** the egg cracking, the wet hatchling, lamp flicker, huge dragon shadows, dust on clothes.

## How hard is each part?

Every item in `assets.json` has a colour:
- **green**: easy.
- **yellow**: doable with care.
- **orange**: hard; keep it simple or keep it short on screen.
- **red**: not possible on this computer.

**Nothing in the plan is red.** Things that would be red, such as true 3D clouds or real reflections everywhere, are swapped for cheaper tricks.

The **orange** items:
- realistic human faces in 4K close-ups (the biggest risk in the whole project), and hair;
- Charcoal, and the general dragon builder;
- the Cling village with its crowd and destruction;
- the stall cloth wrapping over the vendor's face;
- the Santa Maria's ropes and masts.

The render computer is slow: about 57–65 minutes of render time per minute of finished 4K video (measured), so one 4K render of the whole ~25-minute episode takes about a day. So every expensive effect is planned to be as cheap as possible. Dust and mist are among the most expensive things to draw, so they use a few big puffs instead of thousands of small ones.

## Your old engine: what we can reuse

Your old engine (`engine/src`) is its own engine, not three.js, so pieces must be moved over, not just plugged in. I only read it; nothing in it was changed.

| File | What it has | Reuse? | Rough work |
|---|---|---|---|
| `91-face.js` | the sculpted head and 13 face shapes: smile, frown, angry, surprised, sad, blink, open jaw, and 6 mouth shapes for talking | **yes, the most valuable part** | 2–3 days to move, plus 8–12 days of additions |
| `94-human.js` | the body builder: torso, arms, legs, shoes, nose, lips, eyelids, ears | yes | 1–2 days |
| `93-character.js` | automatic skin weights, so the body bends at the joints | yes (not the game controls) | ½–1 day |
| `90-animation.js` | skeleton, two-joint reaching (IK), simple walk/run/idle | partly | 1–1½ days |
| `92-grass.js` | clumpy grass blowing in the wind | yes, for the riding field | 1½–2 days |
| `96-animals.js` | the one-piece body sculpting and the shiny eye trick | the techniques, for dragon bodies | 1–1½ days |
| `87-water.js` | ripples in a pool or tank | only the ripple idea, for the scout's splash | 1–2 days |
| `80-fracture.js` (bonus) | breaking things into chunks | yes, for tiles, wood and the egg | about 1 day |

All together that is about **10–15 days of moving code, saving roughly 3–6 weeks**. These are rough guesses from reading the code.

Four things the old face can't do yet, all needed for close-ups:
1. **The eyes can't look around.** The code works out where to look but never moves the eyes, because they are glued into the head.
2. **There is no inside of the mouth.** Opening the jaw shows skin, not teeth and a dark mouth.
3. **The hands are mittens.** There are no fingers, but the screenplay has close shots of hands holding a rope and holding each other.
4. **Every head is the same face.** There are no sliders yet to make different people, and there are no eyebrows or hair.

## Things noticed in the new pipeline code (not changed)

- The test dragon in `scenes/lib/dragon.js` has **glowing eyes and glowing wings**. That's fine for testing, but it must not be used for real dragons.
- The camera can't get closer than 0.5 m by default, which is too far for face close-ups. Each shot can change this.
- There is no motion blur or depth of field yet. The scout's super-fast pass might want motion blur later.
