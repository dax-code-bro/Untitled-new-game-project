Journal — found beside the body

Something in the warehouse came alive.

I've been hiding here. If anyone finds this, they probably won't. I hear it every day. It keeps walking by. I heard it kill the last of us.

I'm starting to feel dehydrated and starving. There were only a few cans of beans and water when I came here. The beans made this unit smell like garbage, but the water was good to wash it down with.

I've had the last of the water and the beans. I can't go outside to search for more. I tried opening the hallway — that thing came after me. It put its hand straight through me. I managed to run away. I'm bleeding heavily. I don't think I can last much longer.

If anyone comes here — please, I'm begging you. If you escape, call my parents, if you ever do find them.

My name is Nick Ahoy.

Typed page, clipped to a board

I have been here since before there was a company to work for. Six of us, a rented unit, and a loan none of us could cover alone.

Nobody remembers that anymore. They brought in new faces who couldn't tell tungsten from steel, put it on the floor like it was one of us, and called it help. It ran better than any of us ever did. I trained half the people it killed.

It took four months to write. Nights, after the store closed. Not hard, once you understood what had been left inside it by accident — a door held open that nobody meant to leave open. I only had to knock.

I did not want it out. I wanted the doors shut, and everyone behind them, the way I had been shut behind them for eleven years while it got the ribbon-cuttings and the raises and my name stopped coming up in meetings. I did not think about which side of the doors I would end up on.

He was my best friend for eleven years, and I watched them wheel him past that window. I am not going to write his name here.

If you are reading this, the string is still in it somewhere. Do not go looking for it. Whatever answers now isn't listening for that anymore.
