## Placement
- Follows the **M17 coda** (the night of peace; morning = "they've got to find
  him"). The hunt for **Abraham** — the last hostile Pillar — begins.

## STATE OF THE WORLD (new canon)
- **HYDRA IS GONE. ALL of it.** With **Victor Prestige dead** (M17), the
  century-old organization is finished — no owner, no factories, no program.
  The war that consumed two generations is *over*, except for one man:
  **General Abraham**, a continent away in Antarctica.
- So the story pivots from **war** to **hunt** — and from external enemies to
  what the war *left behind* inside the survivors.

## SPIKE — SOMETHING DIRE (new canon, the counterweight scene)

> The happy ending belongs to Molotov. It does **not** belong to Spike.

- **Spike is experiencing something dire.** While everyone else goes home,
  Spike has no home to go to. The peace that lets Molotov finally sleep is the
  same silence that leaves Spike alone with the arithmetic of his life:
  - **He lost the people he looked up to.** Fox. Striker. Payback. The twins.
    All of Team Apex — massacred in front of the world he trusted (M11). His
    father Diego's generation, gone; his own generation, gone.
  - **He's practically a FULL CYBORG.** (⚠ MAJOR canon escalation — see below.)
  - **Everything he's ever loved is gone.** Orphaned by 1985 (Diego and his
    mother both dead). His team dead. His body — piece by piece — gone too.
- **The Molotov/Spike mirror (design note):** the two survivors of Team Apex
  got **opposite endings.** Molotov came out of the fire with a wife, a son,
  a family, a home — *more* than he started with. Spike came out with a
  gravity rig, replacement parts, and a list of graves — *less* of himself
  than he started with, in every sense. The night Molotov finally sleeps is
  the night Spike can't. The happy ending and the dire one are **the same
  night, intercut.**

## ⚠ CANON ESCALATION — SPIKE, THE FULL CYBORG
- **Prior canon:** M10 — Molotov stabbed/shot him; paralyzed ("I can't feel my
  legs"). 2000 era — walks via a **gravity stabilizer waist rig**; heavy
  weapons.
- **NEW (this beat):** by now Spike is **practically a full cyborg** — far
  beyond the waist rig. Over the 15 years, more and more of him was replaced.
- **⚠ OPEN — do NOT invent:**
  - **What parts** are machine (legs certain; arms? torso? more?) and what's
    still flesh.
  - **How it happened** — one event, or gradual (the M10 wounds cascading over
    15 years? battle damage since? did the epilogue-era missions cost him
    parts we haven't scripted?).
  - **Who built him** — Charles (the team's gunsmith/tech) is the obvious
    inference; **confirm.**
  - **How he feels about it** — "dire" suggests the cyborg body is part of the
    crisis (everything he loved is gone — *including his own body*).
- **Thematic note (flag, not canon):** Spike fought a **drone war** and slowly
  became **more machine than the drones he fought.** He is now closer kin to
  **Drone 9** — an aging machine wondering what it's for — than to any living
  member of his team. If the creator wants a Spike↔Nine scene, the rhyme is
  sitting right there. **Not assumed.**
