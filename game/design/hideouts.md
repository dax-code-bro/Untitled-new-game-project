# Hideouts — The Campaign's Explorable Hubs

> **Canon (creator-specified).** The player passes through **THREE different
> hideouts over the main story** — living hub spaces between missions (home
> of the mission board, the crew, and ambient life).

## Hideout 1 — THE MILITARY BASE (1985 era)

> **The BIGGEST hideout in the game.**

### Facilities
- **Five barracks**
- A **super big restroom**
- **Several office buildings**
- A **cafeteria**
- **The garage** — frequently being worked on (mechanics always at it)
- **The missions area** — where **the rest of the crew hangs out** (and
  presumably the mission board lives — confirm)
- **Planes** on-site (flight line), with people inspecting/checking them out
- **Ammunition & gun-building stations** — you can watch armorers building
  ammo and weapons

### Population (headcount canon)
| Who | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Pilots walking around | **~20** |
| Security guards | **10** |
| Military operators | **20** |
| Generals | **10** |
| **The President** | **1** |
| The crew (Team Apex) | in the missions area |

### Ambient life
- **Pilots, security guards, etc. wander the base talking about the most
  random stuff** — overhearable chatter everywhere.
- **Unique voice lines if you TALK to them** — NPCs are directly
  interactable, not just scenery.
- Visible activity: people **checking out stuff on planes**, **building
  ammunition and guns**, working the garage.

### Random interactions (20)
- **~20 different random interactions** can be found around the base —
  e.g., **a few operators getting into trouble**, "and a few other things."
- ⚠ Only the "operators getting into trouble" example was specified; the
  other ~19 interactions are **open** — creator to supply, or delegable
  (say the word and I'll draft the list).

- The base's "1 President" is **the president of the CAMPUS** — the base's
  own civilian head (an administrative "president of the entire campus"),
  **NOT the national President.**
- **Distinct from PRESIDENT JAMES** — *(NEW CANON, named here)* — **the

## Hideout 2 — THE LITTLE MANSION (2000 era) [D details]

> **Creator canon:** the second hideout is **a little mansion.** Details
> below are **[D] delegated designs** (adjustable). Reconciled with story
> canon: this IS the 2000-era HQ — the poor hideout of M13 that gets
> **renovated in M14.**

- **The place:** a small, once-grand mansion — somebody's abandoned estate
  the new team squatted and made theirs. Two floors, a dozen rooms, a
  walled yard gone wild.
- **M13 state (poverty era):** boarded windows, one working faucet, buckets
  under the leaks, furniture burned for heat one bad winter, the chandelier
  sold. Gear degrades because the armory is a damp closet (ties to the
  canon gear-degradation mechanic).
- **M14 state (renovated):** the renovation montage made real — windows
  glazed, generator humming, **Charles's workshop** in the old ballroom
  (gun benches where the dance floor was), the **mission board in the
  dining room** on the wall where a family portrait used to hang.
- **Population:** small and family-shaped — the team (Molotov, Spike,
  Jonah, Nova, Atomic, Jeff, Charles), **Elizabeth** sometimes, nobody
  else. After the base's ~61 NPCs, the emptiness is the point: this era's
  war has seven people and a kid.
- **Random interactions [D] (~10):** Jonah's bottle-target range off the
  back porch (and Molotov silently moving the bottles further each week);
  Atomic bench-pressing the surviving furniture; Charles's bench with a
  "DO NOT TOUCH" sign in five languages; Jeff's door — always closed,
  light always on, never seen entering or leaving; Nova cooking something
  cartel-spiced that makes Spike cry (he claims it's the onions); the
  leak-bucket in the hall everyone steps around like a housemate; the
  team's mismatched mugs, each with a story nobody finishes; a wall where
  someone pencil-marks Jonah's height and someone taller keeps adding
  inches to the top mark as a joke; movie night around one salvaged TV
  with three working channels; Elizabeth's coat hook — sacred, unused for
  weeks at a stretch, and the first thing everyone glances at when they
  come in.
- **Credits note (careful):** the campaign's credits pick Elizabeth up at
  "the mansion" — presumably THIS mansion. *(This identifies the building
  only. 🔒 Sealed question #1 — how/whether Elizabeth got there from
  Antarctica — remains permanently unanswered, per the creator's ruling.)*

## Hideout 3 — THE LITTLE CABIN IN ANTARCTICA (endgame) [D details]

> **Creator canon:** the last hideout is **a little cabin in Antarctica** —
> the M19 "temporary safe house." Details below are **[D] delegated
> designs** (adjustable).

- **The place:** one room and a gear corner. Bunks for five stacked too
  close, a stove nobody needs anymore, frost-proofing on the windows —
  built for the Antarctica that no longer exists. **Outside it's 87° and
  green; inside, a cabin engineered for −40 slowly cooking its occupants.**
  Everyone props the door.
- **The mission board** is a tablet on an ammo crate (the M20 analysis
  tablet — same one).
- **Phase 1 (the expedition):** five men, one secret extra (Hunter beds
  down outside on his own bedroll — walls, he says, are how you get
  cornered), gear stacked to the ceiling, planning by lamplight.
- **Phase 2 (everyone came):** after the family's arrival the cabin is
  hopeless — Elizabeth and Jonah get the bunks, the militia raises a tent
  city in the meadow around it, Martin & Lewis run a quartermaster line
  off folding tables, and the little cabin becomes the war's smallest
  capital: one lamp, one map, thirty tents.
- **Random interactions [D] (~8):** freed prisoners learning rifle drills
  from Jeff (patient) and Atomic (loud); Martin & Lewis inventorying the
  "mountain" of rifles and arguing over the count; a militia man who keeps
  saluting Jonah because the mask looks like rank; someone trying to grow
  a vegetable in Antarctic soil because it's 87° now, why not; Hunter's
  bedroll spot — nobody uses it even when he's away; the sweating-cabin
  joke ("coldest continent on Earth" as everyone fans themselves); a
  nightly map argument about the mountain trail; and Elizabeth and
  Molotov's quiet minute on the porch at sundown that every other
  interaction politely routes around.
